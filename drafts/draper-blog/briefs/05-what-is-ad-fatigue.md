## 2. Consensus

1. **"It fatigued, kill it" is the wrong response.** All eight say so, and it's the one conclusion CONCEPTS.md records as shared. Barry: "I AM BEGGING YOU NOT TO TURN OFF HIGH SPENDING ADS" (`barry-hott/knowledge/evolution.md`, 2026-07-28). Dara: "not to kill the creative" (`5-levels... §Level 2`). Alessio answers with a revival checklist, not a kill (`winners-and-iteration.md §Creative fatigue`). Phil: high-spend, high-CPA ads are not automatic pause candidates (`cpmr-diagnostic.md §4`). Lorenzo: "Spend is a confidence signal" (`evolution.md §11`). Ben: new openings let a body that died at six weeks run "for 6 months or more" (`creative-and-hooks.md`). Marin revives decayed proven ads rather than abandoning them (`creative-diversification-method.md §8`).
2. **Decline is often not about the creative at all.** Barry (activity logs, edit-mode bugs, off-platform checks: `barry-hott/knowledge/themes/testing-and-measurement.md`; `platform-mechanics.md §Executing`). Marin (the answer is inside the ad account "maybe 40% of the time", `cpa-roas-decomposition.md §2`). Phil (decide whether the fix lives inside or outside the account, `cpmr-diagnostic.md §3`). Nick (seasonality and outages first, `platform-changes.md §September 2025 dip`).
3. **Frequency on its own tells you little; read it alongside other numbers.** Nick: 7-day frequency on top spenders, not days since launch (`creative-testing-and-iteration.md §Creative fatigue`). Phil: frequency × CPM, trended, plus net-new reach (`cpmr-diagnostic.md §2`). Marin refuses frequency benchmarks and reads it against spend (`expert-panel/SKILL.md §Refusals`). Dara: rising frequency and a bottom-funnel skew are "the same story told twice" (`16-human-desires... §diagnostic`).
4. **Good ads can run for months or years.** Barry: "ads that have worked for years." Phil: a genuinely scalable ad should hold 6 to 12 months. Alessio: ads running 8 to 12 months, still profitable (`winners-and-iteration.md §Creative fatigue`). Ben: six weeks extended to six months with new hooks. So a fast fade usually means something else is going on.
5. **The lasting fix is new reasons to buy, not more copies.** Marin: new concepts, not new assets, when CTR falls (`cpa-roas-decomposition.md §3`). Nick: market composition, the desire × awareness × persona mix. Dara: build top-of-funnel. Phil: creative diversity is lever 4 of 5. Alessio: fatigue is a production-schedule problem; have replacement versions ready before decline begins.

## 3. Splits

### Split A: what is actually happening when a winner declines? (five ways)
- **Barry, it probably isn't fatigue.** "I don't really buy into the whole fatigue thing… because I have ads that have worked for years" (2024-12-06). He attributes apparent fatigue more to the algorithm penalising non-novel ads than to people burning out. He separates "unicorn" ads from steady "workhorse" ads (2026-07-14). **Action:** leave it on; keep making new ads and improve the post-click experience. Before blaming the platform, check the activity log and placement breakdowns, and watch the ad in its top-spending placement. **Wins when:** the ad is a long-running spender and the decline is ad-level CPA noise. `creative-and-hooks.md §Creative fatigue, §His own diagnosis routine`; `account-structure-and-media-buying.md`.
- **Dara, awareness saturation.** The ad that "falls off a cliff" after 1 to 2 weeks is almost always product- or solution-aware content that exhausted the bottom-funnel audience available to it. **Action:** keep the winner; build top-of-funnel creative to feed it. **Signal:** a sharp spend or CPA decay at 1 to 2 weeks, on an ad classified product/solution-aware. Her frequency warning line is >5 over the trailing week. **Wins when:** the decaying ad is an offer- or comparison-led bottom-funnel ad. `16-human-desires-and-awareness-levels.md` (diagnostic + MCP steps 3 to 4).
- **Alessio, find which part wore out.** Ad fatigue: numb to *this ad*. Footage fatigue: numb to *this footage* reused across angles; fix with new footage on the same script. Face fatigue: numb to *this spokesperson*; fix by sourcing new faces, or removing faces. Revival checklist for a proven winner: refresh visuals, cut or expand sections, add validation, speed up the edit 5 to 8%, reorder, show the product differently. **Wins when:** a proven ad's body still works and one element is worn. `winners-and-iteration.md §Creative fatigue`.
- **Phil, audience saturation, not creative fatigue.** "Creative Fatigue doesn't exist… this should be defined as Audience Fatigue or Audience Saturation" (2023-10-13). The number to trend is cost per 1,000 people reached (CPM × frequency), month over month, alongside net-new reach and new-visitor share. Two failure modes. CPM rising with frequency flat means the auction got pricier (external). Frequency rising with CPM flat means saturation; increase exclusions. **Action, in his order:** measure, exclusions, consolidation, creative diversity, upper funnel. He insists the metric is an indicator, never something to optimise. **Wins when:** the decline is account-wide and builds slowly over months. `cpmr-and-account-health.md`; `cpmr-diagnostic.md §1-6`.
- **Marin, check the measurement before you call it fatigue.** First, is the number real? Compare Meta CPA to blended CPA. Check the view-through share (a red flag above ~25% on 7-day-click/1-day-view). Check for the early-2026 change in what counts as a click. Then work down the metric chain: CPM → CTR → CPC → cost per add-to-cart → cost per checkout → CPA → AOV → CR. Only a CTR drop points to fatigue. His counter-case: an account where the previous buyer blamed fatigue and cycled dozens of concepts, when the real cause was an off-platform change that shifted which buyers the auction went after. When it *is* creative, he blames the classifier: cosmetic variants of one argument get served to the same people. **Wins when:** performance moved and the creative didn't change, or the drop coincides with a tracking, offer or site change. `cpa-roas-decomposition.md §2, §3, §7`; `exclusions-and-account-hygiene.md §When the plumbing is fine`; `creative-diversification-method.md §3`.
- **What the split is really about:** *where* you look first: post-click (Barry), the funnel stage (Dara), the asset (Alessio), the audience pool (Phil), the data (Marin). It isn't five contradictory facts. Each is right for a different symptom.
- **Suggested verdict:** Don't pick one. Turn the five into an ordered checklist, cheapest and most-often-wrong check first (see "Proposed diagnostic order" below).

### Split B: is fatigue real at all?
- **No, or rename it:** Barry (skeptic), Nick (it's a reach ceiling and market composition, plus repetition dulling the response; novelty is the fix: `creative-testing-and-iteration.md §Creative fatigue`), Phil (audience, not creative).
- **Yes, but relocated:** Alessio (three types), Dara (an awareness pool), Ben (the hook, because most viewers never get past the opening), Marin (argument repetition).
- **Suggested verdict:** Define fatigue in the article as "performance decay *attributed* to repetition". Say plainly that the label is often wrong. This is also how CONCEPTS.md defines it.

### Split C: is the fatigue window shrinking?
- **Faster:** Alessio, "Andromeda now burns through creative faster than ever… fatigue hits in days" (2026-03-29). Lorenzo: 6 to 8 weeks two years ago, 10 to 14 days now (in a sponsored article, `ai-in-creative-production.md`).
- **Slower or unchanged:** Alessio's own 8 to 12 month ads (2026-02-24); Phil's 6 to 12 months; Barry's "years".
- **Suggested verdict:** Untested *concepts* fade fast; proven ads can run long. Alessio implies this resolution but never states it. Use it as our reasoning, not as consensus.

### Split D: refresh by duplicating, or leave it alone?
- **Marin:** turn off the decayed proven ad and duplicate it for a fresh start. He claims this works in ~70 to 80% of cases (self-reported). Entry criteria: big past spend *and* past ROAS (`creative-diversification-method.md §8`).
- **Lorenzo:** killing a spending ad throws away its learned history (`testing-win-rate-and-account-discipline.md`).
- **Suggested verdict:** Mention duplication only as "some practitioners", with the entry criteria. The default advice is to leave a spender running and add new creative around it.

### Split E: the cost-per-reach number
Phil's is a full framework. Marin's position is ambivalent: he dismissed it as "basically FREQUENCY" (2026-05), then used it in an audit (2026-08), never reconciled (`marin-istvanic/knowledge/evolution.md §11`). Use the number in the article as one input to trend, never as the target.

## Proposed diagnostic order for the article
1. **Is the drop real?** Compare in-platform CPA/ROAS against backend or blended numbers. Check whether the attribution setting or click definition changed in the window, and keep comparisons on one side of a definition change. Check the activity log for edits, budget changes and accidental changes (Marin, Barry, Phil, Ben all flag the 2026 click redefinition).
2. **Did something outside the ad change?** Offer, price, site, stock, subscription mix, seasonality, outages (Marin's 40/60 rule; Nick's seasonality-first habit).
3. **Where in the funnel does the metric chain break?** CPM up alone points to the auction or placement. CTR down points to the creative. CR down points to the site (Marin).
4. **Is it the ad or the audience pool?** Trend 7-day frequency on top spenders, cost per 1,000 reached, and net-new reach. Rising frequency with flat CPM means saturation, and the fix is exclusions and new audiences (Phil, Nick).
5. **Which buyers did it run out of?** Classify the fading ad's awareness stage. A bottom-funnel winner fading at 1 to 2 weeks needs top-of-funnel support, not replacement (Dara). If the high-frequency ad is itself top-of-funnel, give the same message a new visual format (Lorenzo's branch, usable as reasoning).
6. **Which part wore out?** Opening, footage or face. Refresh that element (Alessio, Ben).
7. **Then act:** keep the spender running, add genuinely new concepts (new reasons to buy), and check blended results after any change (all eight; Lorenzo's 1-to-3-day blended recheck).

## 4. Single-source insights
- **Fatigue is a production-schedule problem (Alessio):** have winning variations ready *before* decline, "so a decline becomes a swap, not a scramble." Sound on first principles; usable as our reasoning.
- **Ranking ads by ROAS favours ads that harvest existing intent (Phil):** high ROAS with high cost-per-reach means a small warm audience is being over-served. Usable as "some practitioners".
- **A hidden dependency (Marin):** a low-ROAS prospecting video may be feeding the conversions a high-frequency retargeting image gets credit for. Kill the video and both die.
- **Read the CTR gap before refreshing (Marin):** a wide gap between all-clicks CTR and link CTR means the copy drives clicks, so vary the image; a narrow gap means vary the copy (`creative-diversification-method.md §6`).
- **Most viewers never see past the opening, so the opening is what wears out (Ben).**

## 5. Numbers
- **7-day frequency warning lines:** >3 on top-5 spenders (Lorenzo) · >3.5 to trigger cost-per-reach work, 5+ with rising cost and low new-visitor share for exclusions (Phil, 2026-01/02) · >5 over the trailing week (Dara). **Range 3 to 5.** Marin and Nick refuse a universal benchmark. Present this as a range, never a rule.
- **Image ads above 2.0 frequency** are reaching warm people (Marin, problem-solving products only).
- **When a winner fades:** 1 to 2 weeks (Dara's "cliff" pattern) · 10 to 14 days (Lorenzo, sponsored) · ~4 weeks (Marin, one brand) · six weeks extendable to 6 months (Ben) · 6 to 12 months for a genuinely scalable ad (Phil) · 8 to 12 months (Alessio) · years (Barry).
- **When to investigate a metric:** a move of ~50% down or ~200% up (Phil, 2026-07-28). New-visitor share ~70% as a target (Phil only).
- **Off-platform share:** the answer sits outside the ad account ~60% of the time (Marin, self-reported).
- **Meta-sourced:** "45% average drop in likelihood to conversion after four repeated exposures" (relayed by Marin; verify and cite Meta directly).

## 6. Myths and retired advice
- **"It fatigued, kill it."** All eight reject it.
- **"Days since launch tells you it's fatigued."** Nick rejects age as the read; use frequency on top spenders.
- **"Ad longevity proves performance."** Retired by Dara in April 2025: an ad can sit live 200+ days in an untouched Advantage+ campaign without converting (`dara-denney/knowledge/themes/platform-mechanics.md`). Long life doesn't prove an ad works, and a fade doesn't prove it's fatigued.
- **"Swap the thumbnail or add an overlay to refresh it."** Cosmetic refreshes now reach the same people (Marin, 2026-08-04; Alessio on filters, 2026-02-24).
- **"Low ROAS on Meta means fatigue."** Measurement artefacts after the 2026 click redefinition (Marin, Barry, Ben, Phil).
- **"Lower your cost per reach" as a goal.** Phil's own guardrail: it's an indicator, not something to optimise.

## 8. Draper angle
- **The point:** the lasting fix for decay is a pipeline of genuinely new concepts, ready before the winner fades. A replacement batch only helps if it goes live fast. Ads Manager makes you rebuild each ad per ad set (17+ clicks per ad built from scratch in one ad set; state that assumption if you multiply it).
- **Naming helps the diagnosis.** Steps 5 and 6 need to know each ad's concept, awareness stage and creator. Draper names each ad from its creative file. Put those fields in the file name and the analysis comes free.
- **Don't** claim Draper detects fatigue or improves performance. It saves the time spent launching; the team spends that time on concepts and diagnosis.
- One inline mention at step 7 ("add new concepts") at most; the standard CTA at the end.
