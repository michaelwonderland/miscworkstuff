## 3. Splits

### Split A: Full-AI ads, especially realistic AI people (avatars, AI UGC)
- **Camp A, "run them, within the compliance line":** Ben builds AI-actor UGC end to end (Arcads, Claude + Meta MCP, 2025-11 and 2026-07) and matches the actor to the customer; Barry ships fully-AI ads and treats AI slop as legitimate; Alessio's 2026-04-12 list of 11 AI concepts includes AI authority-figure avatars and AI speakers. Wins when: speed and volume matter, the offer isn't identity-dependent, and scripts stay third-person.
- **Camp B, "realistic AI loses or is off-limits; real humans for the face of the ad":** Nick (standard UGC still beats AI UGC across hundreds of tests; "if it looks AI… burn it"); Alessio's later 2026-07-26 refinement (realistic AI doesn't compete with a real person shot well; regulators in NY/CA moving); Dara ("full ai ads are generally a disaster"; 2024 refusal of deepfake/UGC-clone tools "for candidly ethical reasons", never retracted); Lorenzo (flagship formats use hired actors, "they're not AI"; "AI UGC… promotes laziness", 2025-01); Phil (AI-generated UGC on a creator platform "is literally not the point", 2026-08-09). Ben himself says identity-dependent offers need the real person.
- **What it's really about:** performance evidence (Nick and Alessio report aggregate losses; Ben shows no A/B against a human control, per his brain's own gaps note), plus ethics and regulatory risk. Note the internal tension in Alessio (AI authority avatar in April, realistic-AI warning in July) and that Nick's dropped "fake-authority AI ads" are close to the AI-dermatologist concept.
- **Suggested verdict:** If the AI is plainly AI (animation, character, voiceover over B-roll), test freely. If it's a realistic synthetic person, treat it as a discovery tool: use it to find the script and angle, then reshoot the winner with a real person. Never let a synthetic person claim personal use or a credential. Don't recommend AI "experts".

### Split B: How far to let agents run the account
- **Camp A, "connect AI to the account and let it act":** Barry runs Meta → Claude → Higgsfield → Claude → Meta to one-shot new ads (2026-04-30); Ben demos Claude launching ads via the Meta MCP, then generating variants of the winner after 5-7 days (2026-07-14); Alessio: agents do ~90% of execution, the strategist the last 10% (2026-03-15).
- **Camp B, "AI stays out of the account; humans decide":** Nick has no AI in buying, bidding or budgets; Marin keys his daily numbers by hand because "I just absorb the numbers better" (2026-05-18) and audits Meta's auto-applied changes; Lorenzo's chain "stops and asks" at every step and "does not know whether an angle worked in your account"; Ben's own timeline: 3-5 years before advertisers trust full automation without pre-approval (2025-07-01).
- **What it's really about:** trust and error cost. Generation errors are cheap to catch; spend and structure errors aren't. Even Camp A keeps a human approval step (Ben approves actor images; Alessio's strategist curates).
- **Suggested verdict:** Let AI draft, generate and report. Keep launch approval, budget changes and kill decisions human. Barry's own prediction that agent connectivity brings "a lot of advertiser volatility" supports the guardrail.

### Split C: Automating analysis and reporting
- **Automate:** Dara (scheduled Cowork reports to Slack; a reputation-analysis agent replacing 3-4 hours per brand); Lorenzo (a Slack agent texting a creative performance analysis every morning, 2026-01-27); Phil (a year of audits fed to ChatGPT for patterns, 2025-06-03).
- **Keep it manual:** Marin (manual entry *is* the review). Barry's and Ben's warnings on confident wrong analysis cut the same way.
- **What it's really about:** working style and what the report is for. Summaries and competitor scans automate well; the judgement layer doesn't (Dara's own value-add step "is not automated").
- **Suggested verdict:** Automate collection and summaries; keep the "what does this mean, what do we do" step human, and check AI claims against the numbers.

### Split D: How much copy AI should write
- Ben: "copywriting, AI does 90 plus percent of it nowadays" (2026-07-25). Nick: AI drafts from a constrained brief, then a human rewrite, never raw. Dara: Claude's copy "kind of sucks"; uses other tools for copy and keeps humans as editors. Lorenzo keeps an (gated) anti-AI-tells file for scripts.
- **Really about:** the input. All agree a briefed, research-fed draft beats a cold prompt. **Verdict:** AI drafts, human edits for voice, claims and "AI tells".

## 4. Single-source insights
