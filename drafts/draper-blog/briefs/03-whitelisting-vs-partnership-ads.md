**Definitions to set up front in the article** (from the corpus, verify against Meta):
- **Partnership ad:** an ad that shows both the brand and the creator in the byline, with the creator's permission granted through Meta's partnership flow (Hub / Creator Marketplace / ad-level toggle). (`CONCEPTS.md` partnership_ad; Ben framework Step 4; Phil §4)
- **Whitelisting:** the brand gets access to run ads from a page or profile that isn't the brand's own, usually a creator's, with the brand's own creative and copy. Sometimes the page is one the brand owns but names around an audience or problem. (Marin playbook §1; Phil §1)

## 2. Consensus

1. **Ads that don't come from the brand page tend to beat ads that do.** Five corpora agree on the direction, by different mechanisms: Marin (borrowed authority, "become the best performers"), Phil ("the ad is not coming from your brand page", handle-only A/B), Dara (partnership ads as the biggest lever), Ben (partnership ad recommended over a plain upload), Nick (third-party page for native ads). Sources: Marin theme §claim; Phil §1-2; Dara theme §Current tactic; Ben framework Step 4; Nick §Native ads.
2. **You don't need a creator budget to start: a non-brand-named page works.** Marin (one "community" page per customer avatar or per problem), Phil (brand-owned page named around an audience or product, "best kept secret"), Dara (a new problem-solution or publication Page, not brand-named, as a free partnership route), Nick (dedicated persona/publication page). Marin theme §own third-party pages; Phil §1, §6; Dara theme §"run for free"; Nick §Native ads.
3. **The founder's own profile is an identity worth running.** Marin (founder stories whitelisted under the founder's profile), Phil (founder content as partnership ads, ~2/week), Dara (founder ads via the founder's IG handle). Same identity, different vehicle. Marin theme §Adjacent; Phil §7; Dara theme.
4. **Buy the asset and the rights, not the post.** Ben ("buy the asset, not the post"; boost permission is the step people skip), Phil (raw footage, full editing rights, no usage time limit; whitelisting is step two of the deal), Marin (negotiate ad rights/access into the paid-post deal from the start). Ben framework Step 2; Phil §5; Marin theme §Sourcing tier 3.
5. **Many creators/pages, not one.** Ben (start with 2-4 creators), Phil (pipeline diversity protects against losing one relationship; concentration framework), Dara (creator variance argues for breadth), Marin (the mechanism needs many pages live at once). Ben Step 1; Phil §3; Dara theme principle 5; Marin playbook §refuses.
6. **Judge it as a programme, with the fee counted.** Phil: 6-8 weeks minimum, most brands quit at 2-3. Marin: fee-adjusted ROAS before renewing access. Ben: creator fee as ~10% of ad budget, justified if it lifts efficiency more than that. Phil §3; Marin theme §economics; Ben theme §economics.

## 3. Splits

### Split A: Which vehicle carries the borrowed identity?
- **Camp A, whitelisting by default, no Partnership Ads (Marin):** "over 80% of our internal brand ads and 20-50% of clients' ads are under whitelisted pages" (2025-05). Refuses the product twice: **2024**, it only promotes an existing creator post and "when you see an ad under 'Brand' with 'Influencer', you know it's a paid post"; **2025**, his creators carry discount codes, so promoting their post means "paying twice". Gains: own DR copy in the creator's voice, many variations behind one handle, the ad never appears on the creator's feed (the objection creators raise), multiple brand ads in one session from different pages. Wins when: creators already earn via affiliate codes, you want copy control and lots of variants, and you can manage page access. Sources: Marin playbook §1; theme §mechanism, §escalation.
- **Camp B, partnership ads, whitelisting retired (Dara):** first question to every brand; whitelisting "phased out"; classic whitelisting only "masked" the creator's handle over brand-pixel targeting with no audience-seed benefit; partnership ads seed delivery from the creator's own audience, which matters most for big spenders whose pixel has narrowed. Wins when: you're spending at scale and need net-new audiences, and creators will grant partnership permission. Sources: Dara theme §Current tactic, §Retired; `evolution.md` §4 (2026-06).
- **Camp C, both, one lever (Phil; Ben leaning B):** Phil runs partnership ads through the Hub with permission at advertiser level (not per-post collaborator codes), plus whitelisting and brand-owned pages, and points spend at **the brand's own audience**, not the creator's. Ben recommends partnership ads over a plain upload but says the targeting benefit is "not as big as some people talk about". Sources: Phil §1, §4, §6; Ben framework Step 4.
- **What the split is really about:** (1) **mechanism**: audience seed (Dara) vs borrowed authority plus multi-page session reach (Marin) vs frequency + relevance on your own audience (Phil); (2) **deal economics**: affiliate codes make boosting the creator's own post double-pay (Marin); (3) **product state**: Marin's 2024 reason (only existing posts) may be out of date if Meta's flow now lets the brand build its own ads with partner identity (Ben's "choose which account it appears from", Phil's advertiser-level access and rebuilt Stories). Verify before the article leans on it.
- **Suggested verdict for the article (an if/then rule):**
  - **New creator relationships: default to partnership ads**, asking for advertiser-level permission so you can build your own ads with their identity, not just boost their post. It's the Meta-supported, labelled route, and Meta is investing in it (Hub, Creator Marketplace merge, performance badge, per Ben/Phil).
  - **Use page-access whitelisting when** the creator is paid by an affiliate code (don't pay twice to boost their post), when you need copy and variant control the partnership flow won't give you, or when you already hold access.
  - **Use a brand-owned audience/problem page when** there's no creator budget yet. Consensus, and it's free.
  - **Whichever vehicle:** run several identities at once, own the raw footage and rights, give it 6-8 weeks, and judge creators with their fee counted.

### Split B: Is whitelisting dead?
- **Dara:** yes, retired (2025-07 framing, 2026-06 mechanism). **Marin, Phil:** live and current in 2026. **Nick:** practises the third-party-page form under a new name (2026-05). **Verdict:** report it as one corpus's retirement, not the panel's. The article should say "some practitioners consider it outdated; others run most of their spend through it" and explain why.

### Split C: How much spend should run through creator identities?
- **Dara:** top brands run 25-50% of Meta budget on partnership-ad content (her case brands, 2025-2026). **Phil:** 5-10% of spend to upper-funnel/partnership activity (2025-06 audit synthesis); 4-6 active partnership pages at ~$40k/month; prescribes 10-20 pages × 5 ads where an account has none (2025-10). **Marin:** >80% internal brand, 20-50% clients under whitelisted pages (2025-05). **What it's really about:** they measure different things (partnership-ad content vs upper-funnel bucket vs any non-brand page). **Verdict:** give the range (single digits to a majority) and the logic: start with a ring-fenced share, grow it as the pipeline produces winners. Don't present any one figure as a benchmark.

### Split D: Whose audience do you target?
- Dara: the creator's audience is the seed. Phil: target your own audience; the creator supplies credibility, not reach. Ben: some delivery benefit, smaller than claimed. Marin: neither, it's authority and multi-page frequency. **Verdict:** article should not promise access to the creator's followers as the reason to do it; position the identity (trust, native feel) as the reliable benefit, audience seeding as a possible extra.

### Adjacent split (mention only if relevant): creators vs hired actors
- Dara treats creators as core; Lorenzo says the creator-testimonial wrapper is exhausted and hires actors (2026-01, re-asserted 2026-07, narrowed 2025-11). This is about **where talent comes from**, not about the vehicle. SKILL "Known live disagreements" row "UGC and creators". Do not average.

## 4. Single-source insights
- **Session stacking** (Marin): from one brand page users see one ad per session; from multiple pages several of your ads can show, so "everybody is using the product". Plausible; verify against Meta delivery docs before stating as fact.
- **Advertiser-level partnership permission beats per-post codes** (Phil, 2025-10 and 2026-02). Operational, checkable.
- **Weekly harvest ritual** of new creator content, and old creator content can still win (Phil §3).
- **Page first, landing page second** for brand-owned pages: validate the page name on an evergreen LP before building a dedicated one; start with statics because the page name carries no brand (Phil §6).
- **Partnership content earns its own campaign** as the exception to consolidation: ad set per creator (5-10+ partners) or per weekly batch (Phil §7).
- **Fee-adjusted ROAS**: add the access fee to spend before computing ROAS; set a reminder before access lapses (Marin theme §economics). The arithmetic is generic and can be shown with invented numbers.
- **Commission as media buying**: a % commission is equivalent to buying at 1/commission ROAS (Marin, 2026-06). Usable as our own arithmetic.
- **Creator sourcing via Meta's Creator Marketplace**: vet on accounts engaged, reach, hook rate, audience demographics; prioritise creators flagged as already doing partnership deals; request extra hook variants in the same shoot (Ben framework Steps 1-3; Dara also cites Marketplace data, `creator-program-system.md`). Two experts, so near-consensus on the tool.
- **Price boost/usage permission separately from content** (Ben). Structure is fine; the figures are off-limits.

## 5. Numbers
All self-reported, single-account or portfolio reads. Use as ranges, never benchmarks.
- **Performance vs brand page:** CPM direction **conflicts**. Marin's internal comparison: ~$1 *higher* CPM, ~$1 lower CPC, +59% 1-day-click new-customer ROAS (one 30-day read reposted three times, 2025-09 → 2026-04). Phil: handle-only A/B, CPM 19% lower, CTR 2x, CPC 11% lower (2026-06); portfolio 40% cheaper cost per purchase (2025-05). Safe article phrasing: "practitioners report higher CTR and cheaper clicks; CPM can go either way."
- **Meta's own figure (primary source, verify):** partnership ads average 13% higher CTR and 71% higher brand lift vs non-partnership ads, as relayed by Ben (2026-06). Cite only if found in Meta documentation, with its URL.
- **Share of spend:** 5-10% (Phil, 2025-06) to 25-50% (Dara, 2025-26) to 80%+ of one internal brand (Marin, 2025-05).
- **Pages / creators:** start with 2-4 creators (Ben); 4-6 active partnership pages at ~$40k/month (Phil); 6-7 brand-owned pages, one per avatar or problem (Marin, two clients).
- **Time to judge:** 6-8 weeks (Phil, 2025-11). Only one expert gives a window.

## 6. Myths and retired advice
- **"Whitelisting is just the old name for partnership ads."** Contested in `CONCEPTS.md`: two corpora run whitelisting as a different mechanic today.
- **"Buy a post with 60 days of usage."** Phil's failure case and Ben's "48 hours" point: own raw footage and rights instead (Phil §5; Ben framework intro).
- **"Quit after 2-3 weeks if it isn't working."** Phil: most brands do, and that's why it "fails" (§3, 2025-11).
- **Follower-count tiers as the selection rule.** Dara retired micro/nano/macro classification (theme §Retired); Ben vets on engagement and fit, not followers.
- **Collaborator codes at the ad level.** Phil: use Hub requests for advertiser-level access instead (2026-02). Verify against current Meta UI.
- **"One creator page is a whitelisting programme."** Marin: the mechanism needs many pages (playbook §refuses).
- Dara's own "UGC is still king" (2024) is retired in her corpus in favour of partnership-ad activation (evolution §4); not central here.
