## 2. Consensus

1. **UGC describes where the video came from (a non-brand person, their voice, their setting), not how it's run.** It can run from the brand's page, a creator's handle or a third-party page. (CONCEPTS `creative.creator_content`; Phil: "Video creative does not mean UGC", `video-ugc-and-creators.md §principle 2`; Nick: UGC as "a mini production studio" usable inside any style, `creative-formats.md §tier list`)
2. **UGC creators and influencers are different things.** Ben: UGC is an ordinary person with no following, influencer content buys audience and borrowed trust. Dara: four tiers by how each builds community (UGC creator → creator → influencer → celebrity), with UGC creators weakest on community. (`ben-heath/.../creators-and-partnership-ads.md`; `dara-denney/.../creator-program-system.md §A`)
3. **It works because it doesn't look like an ad.** Barry, Alessio, Phil, Ben, Nick and Lorenzo all say it; they split on cost (Split B). (`barry-hott/.../ugly-ads.md §Why it works`; `alessio-cordeddu/.../creative-psychology.md §1`; `phil-kiel/.../video-ugc-and-creators.md §principle 5`; `ben-heath/.../creative-and-hooks.md`; `lorenzo-pravata/.../stealth-creatives-and-the-native-feed-doctrine.md`)
4. **The person on camera is the biggest variable.** Dara: "Nine times out of 10, it actually comes down to the creator"; Ben: same offer, different person, very different ROAS; Lorenzo: casting is "the only line item worth paying up for"; Alessio: flat talent makes people scroll. (`dara-denney/.../creators-and-partnership-ads.md §principle 2`; `ben-heath/.../creators-and-partnership-ads.md §evidence`; `lorenzo-pravata/.../ugc-actors-and-production-standards.md`; `alessio-cordeddu/.../creative-psychology.md §Casting`)
5. **Use several creators, not one.** Dara: the same creator varies wildly clip to clip, so you need several and longer partnerships; Ben: start with 2-4; Phil: test 2-3 new creators a month while a cornerstone runs; Alessio: keep sourcing new faces. (`creator-program-system.md §B`; `ben-heath/.../creators-and-partnership-ads.md §Selection`; `phil-kiel/.../video-ugc-and-creators.md §concentration`; `alessio-cordeddu/.../winners-and-iteration.md`)
6. **Buy the raw footage and the paid-usage rights, not a post.** Ben: an organic post lives 48 hours; Phil: negotiate raw footage, editing rights and no time limit; Nick: always buy raw footage for a content bank; Dara: raw assets plus a rough edit. (`ben-heath/.../creators-and-partnership-ads.md §buy the asset`; `phil-kiel/.../video-ugc-and-creators.md §Own the raw footage`; `nick-theriot/.../creative-formats.md §UGC sourcing`; `dara-denney/.../creator-program-system.md` Caraway row)
7. **Get more ads out of each shoot before reshooting.** Phil iterates a talking-head before reshooting (new hooks, b-roll over the middle, mashups); Nick cuts one raw clip into ~6 variants; Ben asks for extra hooks in the same shoot; Dara and Alessio swap the setting with green screen and the face/creator as iteration levers. (`phil-kiel/.../yapper-talking-head.md`; `nick-theriot/.../creative-formats.md`; `ben-heath/.../creators-and-partnership-ads.md §Briefing`; `dara-denney/.../authenticity-framework-sops.md §SOP 1`)
8. **Realistic AI UGC isn't beating real people yet, and no AI or hired person may claim personal use.** Nick: standard UGC beats AI UGC in aggregate (mid-2026); Alessio: "We're not seeing realistic AI even compete with the real person shot the right way"; Dara: full AI ads "generally a disaster", mixed media works. Barry and Ben independently draw the first-person line. (`nick-theriot/.../creative-formats.md §AI creative`; `alessio-cordeddu/.../ai-and-tooling.md`; `dara-denney/knowledge/evolution.md §1`; `barry-hott/.../ai-and-tooling.md §compliance line`; `ben-heath/.../ai-and-tooling.md`)

## 3. Splits

### Split A: Are creators core, or is the UGC wrapper exhausted? (documented, current)
- **Camp A, creators are core:** Dara: "If you want to have a shot in hell in winning meta ads over the next few years, then you need a creator program" (2026-08); Meta itself says future selling is creator-led. Ben: influencers graded S in 2026, "the single recommendation that is ignored the most". Phil: talking-head UGC carries most spend in some 2026 accounts. Nick: standard UGC "still one of our highest converting ad styles" for proof-dependent products. Marin: "no format is dead" (2026-06). Wins when: you can source and keep good creators; proof-dependent products; brands spending enough that reaching new people is the constraint. (`dara-denney/.../creator-program-system.md`; `ben-heath/.../creators-and-partnership-ads.md`; `phil-kiel/.../video-ugc-and-creators.md`; `nick-theriot/.../creative-formats.md §7 levels`; `marin-istvanic/.../creative-strategy-and-diversification.md`)
- **Camp B, the wrapper is exhausted, hire actors:** Lorenzo: "UGC is dead in 2026. To scale you need new, diverse formats" (2026-07-26); "Most brands pay influencers. Smart brands hire actors" (2026-01); win rate up since switching from UGC creators to trained actors (2025-12, no numbers). His operative claim is narrower than the slogan: one person to a phone, product in frame, "I'm obsessed" copy is saturated; he still published a UGC template in 2026-03 and said "UGC didn't die. Lazy scriptwriting did" (2025-11). Wins when: the category is saturated with identical creator testimonials, budget allows studio production, formats need two people (podcast, interview, skeptic/believer). (`lorenzo-pravata/.../ugc-actors-and-production-standards.md`; `evolution.md §1, §9`)
- **Partial support for B's diagnosis, not its cure:** Ben downgraded generic UGC to B in 2026 ("not as effective as it used to be") but his fix is *more* audience (influencers), not actors. Dara retired "UGC is still King" (2024) and calls the generic UGC testimonial a faded format; her fix is creators with real community, run as partnership ads, plus looser "yapper" delivery. (`ben-heath/knowledge/evolution.md §7`; `dara-denney/.../evolution.md §4`)
- **Really about:** where the talent comes from and what it costs. Everyone agrees the generic, scripted, one-person "I'm obsessed" testimonial has worn out; they disagree on the replacement: creators with community (Dara, Ben), looser talking-head delivery (Dara, Phil), or trained actors in a studio (Lorenzo).
- **Verdict:** the article should say **generic UGC is tired; UGC isn't dead**. If your creator ads all look the same, the fix is a different person, a different structure or a different vehicle, not deleting creators. If you can't find creators who are believable without a script, actors are a legitimate answer. Never write "UGC is dead" as fact; name it as a contested claim.

### Split B: Cheap and native, or native-looking but expensive? (documented)
- **Camp A, cheap/native:** Barry: a deliberately unpolished look reads as real; "ugly" means real, not bad; no cost floor. Alessio: most top performers shot on phones, "Performance is messy" (audio the exception). Phil: native creator content is "faster and easier to produce". Ben: low-production native graded A for low-ticket e-commerce. (`barry-hott/.../ugly-ads.md`; `alessio-cordeddu/.../creative-psychology.md`; `phil-kiel/.../video-ugc-and-creators.md`; `ben-heath/.../creative-and-hooks.md`)
- **Camp B, stealth is high-production:** Lorenzo passed through "ugly ads work" (2024-09) and moved on: studio, actors, 2-3 cameras, good mics; "If your podcast style ads are low budget, it will damage your brand more than you think" (2024-11); "Cheap creatives get cheap results" (2026-01). (`lorenzo-pravata/.../stealth-creatives-and-the-native-feed-doctrine.md §The production standard`)
- **Camp C, match to the stakes:** Ben (UGC for low-stakes, polish for medical/financial), Nick (videographer for high-perceived-value), Phil (premium all the way down), Dara (high-AOV brands buy higher-production creator content). (Sources in brief 07 Split C.)
- **Really about:** production cost vs price point. All three reject the polished brand ad.
- **Verdict:** if/then by ticket size. Low-ticket: phone-shot creator content, spend the money on more creators. High-ticket or high-trust: keep the native look, pay for casting, sound and setting. Never equate "native" with "cheap" or "polished" with "premium".

### Split C: Script it, or let the creator talk?
- **Loose:** Ben: specific on structure (hooks, CTA), loose on script; strict brands "don't do as well". Nick: a hook plus three talking points; over-scripting "sounds like you're a robot". Barry: rigid briefs and brand books make all output converge. Dara: the yapper format needs a script loose enough to adapt; affiliate creators get no brief. Phil: for talking-heads, "don't ship them the product" so the creator focuses on the talk. (`ben-heath/.../creators-and-partnership-ads.md §Briefing`; `nick-theriot/.../creative-formats.md §UGC sourcing`; `barry-hott/.../creative-resourcing-90-10.md §5`; `dara-denney/.../creator-program-system.md §D`; `phil-kiel/.../video-ugc-and-creators.md`)
- **Tight:** Dara: full clip-by-clip storyboards remain standard for UGC creators; "scripting is not retired". Phil (2026-08): "Write script → becomes top spender → give script to new creator… Old school scripted UGC, still gold": the script is the reusable asset. Lorenzo: printed scripts, two actors, the fault is "lazy scriptwriting". (`creator-program-system.md §D`; `phil-kiel/.../video-ugc-and-creators.md §The script → creator pipeline`; `lorenzo-pravata/.../ugc-actors-and-production-standards.md`)
- **Really about:** who can deliver a script naturally. Dara's rule resolves most of it: **brief depth inverse to the creator's tier**: full storyboard for new UGC creators, a lighter brief for influencers, often none for affiliates.
- **Verdict:** script the structure and the proof; free the delivery. A tight script needs someone who can act it (actor or proven creator); a loose brief needs a creator who already "has it". A winning script is worth re-shooting with new faces.

### Split D: Partnership ads, whitelisting, or third-party pages? (three-way; detail in article 03)
- **Dara:** partnership ads are the #1 lever (seed delivery from the creator's audience); whitelisting is phased out ("not your 2022 advice"). **Phil:** both are live, partnership ads "white hat", whitelisting "black hat", plus brand-owned second pages. **Marin:** whitelisting (direct page access, his own copy) is his default; refuses the Partnership Ads product because creators' discount codes mean "paying twice". **Ben:** run as a partnership ad, but the delivery benefit is "not as big as some people talk about". **Nick:** renamed his "whitelisting" to native ads on a third-party page. Barry, Alessio, Lorenzo: silent. (`dara-denney/.../creator-program-system.md`; `phil-kiel/.../partnership-whitelisting-flywheel.md §1`; `marin-istvanic/.../whitelisting-and-partnership-ads.md`; `ben-heath/.../creators-and-partnership-ads.md §operational details`; `nick-theriot/.../creative-formats.md §Native ads`)
- **Agreement underneath:** an ad from a person or an unbranded page beats one from the brand page (borrowed trust). Dara, Phil and Marin all also run unbranded "publication" or "community" pages.
- **Verdict for this article:** define the three terms in one short section, state that practitioners disagree on mechanics and economics, link to 03. **Don't say Draper supports partnership ads.**

### Split E: AI avatars
- **Use, with rules:** Ben runs AI actors (an affiliate-linked recommendation; disclose as vendor demo) with the no-personal-use line and "match the actor to the customer". Barry: AI is a production tool; same compliance line as for paid humans. (`ben-heath/.../ai-and-tooling.md`; `barry-hott/.../ai-and-tooling.md`)
- **Test with AI, ship real people:** Alessio: "Test the briefs with AI, find what converts, then shoot the winners with real people"; animation is "honest about being fake" and safe; realistic AI flagged for regulation (NY, CA). Nick: run AI only if it doesn't look AI; standard UGC still wins. (`alessio-cordeddu/.../creative-flooding.md`, `ai-and-tooling.md`; `nick-theriot/.../creative-formats.md §AI creative`)
- **Against where the human is the point:** Lorenzo: "AI UGC has one fatal problem: It promotes laziness" (2025-01); his studio formats are explicitly not AI. Phil: creators submitting AI content is "literally not the point". Dara: avoids deepfake/UGC-clone tools "for candidly ethical reasons" (2024, never restated or retracted); likes AI that is honest about being AI. (`lorenzo-pravata/knowledge/evolution.md §8`; `phil-kiel/.../video-ugc-and-creators.md`; `dara-denney/knowledge/evolution.md §1`)
- **A conflict to keep out of the article:** Alessio lists an AI "authority-figure avatar" (e.g. a retired dermatologist) among his 2026 AI concepts; Nick treats fabricated authority as fraud exposure. Don't recommend synthetic experts.
- **Verdict:** AI avatars are for testing scripts and angles cheaply, and for formats that are obviously not real (animation). For the final ad, real people still win in the corpus; and no synthetic person says "I used this".

## 4. Single-source insights
- **The "has it" screen in 2-3 seconds**: vocal variety, magnetism, confidence without overcompensating; red flags are monotone script-reading and diving into features with no story (Dara). Usable as plain reasoning. (`creator-program-system.md §B`)
- **Recruit by identity, not product** ("calling all women over 30"), and cast for fit, not for whoever replies first (Nick). (`creative-formats.md §UGC sourcing`)
- **Track the setting as a variable in ad names** (a good kitchen behind the creator) (Phil). Direct tie to Draper naming. (`video-ugc-and-creators.md`)
- **Three kinds of burn-out**: the ad, the footage, and the face; remove or rotate faces to extend life (Alessio). Describe, don't name. (`winners-and-iteration.md`)
- **One creator carrying 40-60% of revenue is common; diversify gradually, don't cut** (Phil). (`video-ugc-and-creators.md §concentration`)
- **In-house creators** posting daily to a brand-owned handle as a growth lever (Dara). Mention as an option only; no pay figures.
- **Keep strategist and creator-sourcing roles separate** (Dara).
- **"List what you least want to do; that's roughly impact order"**: hiring creators is the example (Ben).
- **Several creator pages let several of your ads reach one person in a session** (Marin; his delivery claim, unverified).
- **Credential reveal at the end, middle-income settings by default** (Nick).

## 5. Numbers
- **Cost per creator video:** roughly $100 to $1,000 across corpora (Ben: ~$100 for small niche creators; Nick: ~$200-300 sweet spot, market $80-350, raw-footage packages up to ~$800; Dara: $250-1,000 per raw piece). Use only as a wide range "from about $100 to about $1,000 depending on the creator and the rights"; no individual figure.
- **Creators to start with:** 2-4 (Ben); 2-3 new per month alongside a cornerstone (Phil). Range: start with a few, keep adding.
- **Time before judging a creator/partnership program:** 6-8 weeks (Phil). Single source; reasoning only.
- **Variants per raw clip:** ~6 (Nick). Single source; fine as "several".
- **Creator content production timeline:** 3-4 weeks hire → launch (Nick). Single source.
- **Share of spend on creator/partnership content:** only Dara gives a benchmark (25-50%, from brands she interviewed). Not usable as a figure.
- **No UGC CTR/CPA/ROAS benchmark** in any corpus.

## 6. Myths and retired advice
- **"UGC is king" (2024).** Retired by Dara and Ben (Ben's "UGC style video is king" 2025-09 → B grade 2026-07). The live claim is "the right creator", not "UGC as a style". (`dara-denney/.../evolution.md §4`; `ben-heath/.../evolution.md §7`)
- **"UGC is dead."** Not a settled fact: one expert's dated, self-contradicting position, opposed by five corpora. (Split A)
- **"Scripted UGC is dead."** Dara and Phil both correct it. (Split C)
- **Micro / nano / macro by follower count.** Retired by Dara (Andromeda makes follower count matter less); Ben vets on engagement and fit, not followers. (`creator-program-system.md §A`; `ben-heath/.../creators-and-partnership-ads.md §Selection`)
- **One-off "churn and burn" creator deals.** Retired by Dara; replaced by retainers and longer partnerships. Phil's "~90% of brands quit after 2-3 weeks" is the same lesson from the other side.
- **"Whitelisting is just the old name for partnership ads."** Contested (CONCEPTS `creative.partnership_ad`): Phil and Marin run whitelisting as a different mechanic. Don't repeat the simplification.
- **"People buy UGC because the creator looks like them."** Dara calls the explanation wrong (2025-03); Ben and Alessio still cast to resemble the buyer. Present as: resemblance helps, delivery decides. (Brief 07 Split E)
- **"AI UGC will replace creators."** No expert holds it; the corpus says real people still win on realistic formats (mid-2026).

## 8. Draper angle
PLAN gives none (12 links to this one). Defensible from the profile: **creator content multiplies the ads you have to launch.**
- The consensus moves are all multipliers: several creators, several hooks per shoot, ~6 cuts per raw clip, reshoot a winning script with new faces. One shoot becomes a batch; the batch has to reach every ad set. That's "create once, deploy many". Do the arithmetic openly (e.g. 3 creators × 6 cuts × 4 ad sets × 17 clicks, stating the rebuild-per-ad-set assumption, or `[TK]` the duplicate cost).
- **Automatic ad naming** from the file: put creator, hook and setting in the file name so the later read shows which person and which cut carries spend (the "setting as a variable" idea). Links to 02.
- **Cloud-native creative**: raw creator footage usually lands in a shared Google Drive; launch from there.
- Belief 4 ("empower, don't replace") fits the AI-avatar section: AI makes test creative cheap; the team's time should go to casting and briefing real people.
- **Partnership ads are not a Draper feature.** Don't imply Draper launches them or manages creator permissions.
- Cross-links: 07 (testimonials), 03 (whitelisting vs partnership), 12 (AI), 05 (fatigue).
