```yaml
experts_consulted: [barry-hott, dara-denney, phil-kiel, nick-theriot, alessio-cordeddu, ben-heath, marin-istvanic, lorenzo-pravata]
```

Routing note: "testimonial" sits across three objects in the corpora: (1) the **words** (a customer's line, mined from reviews or comments), (2) the **format** that carries them (quote static, talking-head review, reaction compilation, before/after), and (3) the **messenger** (real customer, paid creator, actor, AI avatar). Most disagreement lives in (2) and (3); there is near-consensus on (1). The creator-vs-actor fight belongs mainly to brief 10 (UGC); here it only matters where it changes who delivers a testimonial. No corpus publishes a testimonial-specific benchmark.

## 2. Consensus

1. **The best testimonial line is found, not written: mine reviews, comments and customer conversations for the customer's own words.** Dara, Lorenzo, Nick, Barry and Marin all make customer language the source. (`dara-denney/.../golden-nugget-review-mining.md`; `lorenzo-pravata/.../research-and-customer-language.md §Review mining`; `nick-theriot/.../customer-research.md`; `barry-hott/.../post-it-ideation.md` step 3; `marin-istvanic/.../creative-diversification-method.md` step 2. Phil's brief template asks for the customer's emotion but the review-mining step in his file is the brain's execution note, not his stated doctrine: don't count him.)
2. **Specific beats glowing.** Generic praise ("highly recommend", "loved by customers") carries nothing; numbers, timeframes, ages, odd or taboo details and named failed alternatives carry the ad. (Dara's nugget criteria, `golden-nugget-review-mining.md §What counts`; Alessio, `creative-psychology.md §7` "'Loved by customers' doesn't"; Lorenzo's verbatim rule, `research-and-customer-language.md`; Phil "Specificity is what makes people feel understood", `video-ugc-and-creators.md §Current tactic`; Ben, testimonials "with specific results attached", `creative-and-hooks.md §Proof`)
3. **Keep the words raw. A quote polished enough for a billboard stops reading as a customer.** Alessio: best cards "sound like a person texting a friend"; Lorenzo: verbatim, "the typos stay in"; Dara: nuggets work because a copywriter would never have written them, and never use brand fonts in UGC-style creative. (`alessio-cordeddu/.../static-ads.md` #8; `lorenzo-pravata/.../research-and-customer-language.md`; `dara-denney/.../golden-nugget-review-mining.md`, `themes/ad-formats.md §editing craft`)
4. **The skeptic who came round outsells the enthusiast.** Three independent routes to it: Dara builds objection-led creative around testimonials that name the customer's initial doubt; Phil's "reluctant converts are more believable than enthusiastic ones" (premium products); Lorenzo casts a skeptic who "comes around honestly" as the one line item worth paying for. (`dara-denney/.../authenticity-framework-sops.md §SOP 2`; `phil-kiel/.../video-ugc-and-creators.md §The script → creator pipeline`; `lorenzo-pravata/.../ugc-actors-and-production-standards.md §The production standard`)
5. **Lead with the objection, especially price.** Dara: price is the most common objection, reframe it as a benefit; Phil: "The price is the hook, not the obstacle"; Nick: the deliberately negative review whose complaint is the benefit is S-tier. (`authenticity-framework-sops.md §SOP 2 Step 4`; `video-ugc-and-creators.md`; `nick-theriot/.../creative-formats.md §tier list`)
6. **One voice per ad beats a wall of testimonials** (for video testimonials and creator content). Dara: single-creator testimonials outperformed compilations by 2024; Alessio: stacking proof has "diminishing returns, sometimes even negative returns", split ten testimonials into ten single-person ads. Ben's reaction compilations are the exception (see Split A). (`dara-denney/.../ad-formats.md §Retired`; `alessio-cordeddu/.../ad-anatomy-and-scripting.md §Retired`)
7. **It must not look like an ad.** Barry, Alessio, Phil, Ben, Nick and Lorenzo all state this as the goal; they split only on what it costs (Split C). (`barry-hott/.../ugly-ads.md`; `alessio-cordeddu/.../creative-psychology.md §1`; `phil-kiel/.../creative-strategy-and-diversity.md`; `ben-heath/.../creative-and-hooks.md §Reels-first` "make the ad not look like an ad"; `lorenzo-pravata/.../stealth-creatives-and-the-native-feed-doctrine.md`)
8. **No one may claim personal experience they don't have.** An AI character or hired actor can endorse ("this problem sucks, try this") but not say "I used it and it worked". Barry and Ben state it independently; Nick extends it to fabricated credentials (fraud exposure) and says even a *true* testimonial with a specific weight/timeframe claim is risky. (`barry-hott/.../ai-and-tooling.md §The compliance line`; `ben-heath/.../ai-and-tooling.md §AI-generated actors`; `nick-theriot/.../compliance-and-ethics.md §Claim language, §Fabricated authority`)

## 3. Splits

### Split A: Stack testimonials, or one per ad?
- **Camp A, one voice per ad:** Alessio (2026-02, restated a month later): stacked proof reads as "defending yourself in court"; medium proof slightly beat high proof in his creative analysis. Dara: compilations retired as a standalone format; they work only combined with another format. Wins when: creator or customer talking-head testimonials, crowded categories where viewers are already skeptical. (`alessio-cordeddu/.../ad-anatomy-and-scripting.md`; `dara-denney/.../ad-formats.md`)
- **Camp B, proof-heavy and compilations can work:** Ben's anchor case is a compilation of ~5-second clips of clients' immediate post-treatment reactions, offer only in the copy, and "every single ad that I have live has proof in it… 80, 90%… or 25%". Wins when: the category is already understood (his own caveat), reactions are short and genuine, local/service businesses. (`ben-heath/.../creative-and-hooks.md §Proof as a creative strategy`, 2024-10 foundational)
- **Really about:** the unit of proof. Ben's clips are reactions, not stacked spoken testimonials, and his case is a local service. Not a direct contradiction.
- **Verdict:** default to one customer, one story, one ad, and make ten ads from ten testimonials. Use a fast reaction compilation only when the viewer already knows what the service does and the clips are seconds long.

### Split B: Before/after testimonials
- **Dara:** "Show the transformation, don't describe it"; transformation statics are "the single biggest gap she finds in ad accounts"; before/afters are **not illegal**, with extra restrictions in cosmetic-procedure and weight-loss; use unedited footage, a day-1 vs day-30 progression works. In video, before/after is not a standalone format. (`dara-denney/.../static-ad-construction.md`; `themes/ad-formats.md`)
- **Alessio (2024 vintage, dated by the corpus itself):** split-screen before/after is "perfectly fine… approved daily" if results and timeframes are realistic; the risk is bold promises. Before/after and timeline-proof statics are in his 2026 concept list. (`alessio-cordeddu/.../research-and-competitor-intel.md`; `static-ads.md` #6-7)
- **Ben:** use **sequential**, not split-image, before/afters for body transformation because Meta restricts it. (`ben-heath/.../creative-and-hooks.md §Proof`, 2024-10)
- **Nick:** downgraded to C tier; an acne client test found after-only "way better" than before/after; keep before/afters on the product page; bans face, stomach, teeth and hair before/afters. (`nick-theriot/.../creative-formats.md §Before/after`; `compliance-and-ethics.md §Claim language`, 2026-01)
- **Really about:** category and policy exposure, plus one account test. Genuine disagreement on performance (Dara/Alessio up, Nick down) and on format (split vs sequential).
- **Verdict:** if/then. Body, skin, hair, teeth or weight categories: show the *after* or a time progression, keep claims to realistic averages, and check Meta's current policy (open question). Elsewhere (home, cleaning, product function): a before/after transformation is one of the strongest proof formats; test it.

### Split C: Ugly or polished testimonials?
- **Camp A, cheap and native:** Barry (ugly ads "more important than ever", 2026-07; ugly means real, not bad), Alessio (most top performers shot on phones; audio is the one non-negotiable), Phil ("Performance > polish"; native is cheaper to make). (`barry-hott/.../ugly-ads.md`; `alessio-cordeddu/.../creative-psychology.md`; `phil-kiel/.../creative-strategy-and-diversity.md`)
- **Camp B, native-looking but expensive:** Lorenzo: studio, two actors, multiple cameras, good audio; "If your podcast style ads are low budget, it will damage your brand"; casting the believable skeptic is where money goes. (`lorenzo-pravata/.../stealth-creatives-and-the-native-feed-doctrine.md §The production standard`)
- **Camp C, match production to the stakes:** Ben: UGC wins low-stakes purchases, studio polish wins high-stakes (medical, financial); Nick: pay for a videographer in high-perceived-value categories; Phil: "premium has to be premium all the way down"; Dara: high-AOV brands keep higher-production creators on retainer. (`ben-heath/.../creative-and-hooks.md §Production value must match positioning`; `nick-theriot/.../creative-formats.md §craft`; `phil-kiel/.../video-ugc-and-creators.md`; `dara-denney/.../creator-program-system.md §E`)
- **Really about:** production cost and price point, not taste. Everyone rejects the glossy brand-voice ad.
- **Verdict:** the *words* stay raw everywhere (consensus 3). For production: low-ticket and impulse products, shoot on a phone; high-ticket or high-trust (health, finance, premium), invest in casting, sound and setting while keeping the conversational look. Cheap and native or expensive and native are both live; glossy and branded is the one losing option.

### Split D: Reviews first, or reviews last?
- **Reviews first:** Dara, Lorenzo, Nick (own and competitor 1-3 star), Barry (competitor reviews). (Sources in consensus 1.)
- **Reviews last:** Alessio puts customer feedback last in onboarding "because it's not usually where I find innovation": existing reviews are selected by existing messaging. (`alessio-cordeddu/.../research-and-competitor-intel.md §Step 1`)
- **Also:** Barry says negative ad comments beat reviews, because reviews only capture buyers; Dara and Nick treat ad comments as a separate, necessary source for objections. (`barry-hott/.../creative-and-hooks.md` 2024-03; `dara-denney/.../golden-nugget-review-mining.md §Where to mine`)
- **Verdict:** reviews for the *lines* (testimonial copy), comments and Reddit for the *objections* the testimonial must answer. Alessio's warning is worth one sentence: reviews only reflect the people your current ads already convinced.

### Split E: "People buy from people who look like them"?
- **Still true:** Ben ("people buy from people that look and sound like them"); Alessio's relatability stack (actor looks like the buyer, buyer's setting); Nick casts for demographic mirror. (`ben-heath/.../ai-and-tooling.md`; `alessio-cordeddu/.../creative-psychology.md §2`; `nick-theriot/.../creative-formats.md §UGC sourcing`)
- **The explanation is wrong:** Dara (2025-03): "the jig is up on that one"; trust in the specific person and their delivery converts. She still requires creator diversity across personas. (`dara-denney/.../creators-and-partnership-ads.md §principle 1`)
- **Verdict:** match the messenger to the buyer, but a lookalike with flat delivery still loses. Delivery and specificity first, resemblance second.

## 4. Single-source insights
- **Name and town on local testimonials** (Ben, local-business, 2024): in-group relevance. The only corpus that addresses displaying names. (`ben-heath/.../lead-gen-and-local-business.md`)
- **Reviewer's age as a marker of a usable line** (Dara). (`golden-nugget-review-mining.md`)
- **Put the credential reveal at the end** of a testimonial video, not the start (Nick). (`creative-formats.md §craft`)
- **Launch by post ID to keep accumulated comments and likes**, social proof that travels with the ad, even into new countries (Phil). (`multi-country-scaling.md`)
- **Hide negative comments on top spenders** (Barry, 2023-24 foundational, not restated since; date it). (`barry-hott/.../creative-and-hooks.md`)
- **Review-count sanity**: claimed review counts that don't match visible reviews break trust; roughly 1 review per 10 orders (Nick). "Verified buyer" only for real orders. (`compliance-and-ethics.md`)
- **Founder records calls with best customers and runs them as ads** (Alessio, 2026-04). (`alessio-cordeddu/knowledge/themes/ai-and-tooling.md` founder formats)
- **Real customer videos have a brutal hit rate** (Barry, 2024: a handful usable out of hundreds). Use as reasoning ("most raw customer videos won't work; collect many"), not as a figure.
- **The "failed attempts" review bucket** is the richest source of hooks (Dara). Lorenzo's language bank has a "failed solutions" group too, so this is near-consensus on the idea. (`golden-nugget-review-mining.md`; `research-and-customer-language.md`)
- **Many pages, one session**: ads from several creator pages can reach one person in one session and create an "everybody is using it" effect (Marin; his claim about delivery, unverified). (`whitelisting-and-partnership-ads.md`)

## 5. Numbers
- **Proof share inside an ad:** 25% to 80-90% (Ben, 2024). Single source; reasoning only.
- **Ad length for testimonial-style video:** Alessio's 2026 analysis favours 25-35 seconds; Barry holds "no ad is too long, only too boring" and cites a multi-minute testimonial round-table winner. No consensus; don't give a target length.
- **Review sample size for mining:** Lorenzo wants 200+ reviews (worked from ~93); Dara sampled 3,000 of a very large review base. Range: from a couple of hundred up to a few thousand is plenty.
- **Shortlist output:** Dara's mining pass ends in 10-20 candidate lines. Single source; fine as practical guidance.
- **Press/authority logos on a proof static:** 3-4 max (Alessio). Single source.
- **No testimonial CTR/CPA benchmark exists in any corpus.** Don't invent one.

## 6. Myths and retired advice
- **"A competitor's ad has run 200+ days, so it's a winning testimonial to copy."** Retired by Dara (2025-04): long-running ads may sit untouched in Advantage+. Ben notes the Ads Library sort changed in 2026, so the longevity heuristic is unreliable. Highly relevant for an "examples" article. (`dara-denney/knowledge/evolution.md §3a`; `ben-heath/knowledge/frameworks/ads-library-modeling.md`)
- **"Testimonial compilations are a format."** Retired as standalone by Dara (by 2024). (`ad-formats.md §Retired`)
- **"Stack as much proof as possible."** Called old gospel by Alessio, 2026-02. (`ad-anatomy-and-scripting.md`)
- **"Before/after ads are banned."** Myth (Dara corrects it repeatedly; Alessio too). The truth is category restrictions. (Split B)
- **"I'm obsessed" as the opener.** Phil's own mid-2025 winner opened that way; by 2026-08 he names it the worst option for premium products specifically. Don't generalise beyond premium. (`video-ugc-and-creators.md`)
- **"Generic UGC testimonial" as a headline format:** faded across Dara's annual format lists (2024 → 2026). (`annual-format-list-2026.md`)
- **"Design for sound off"**: reversed by Dara (2024) and Ben (2025). (`ad-formats.md §Retired`; `ben-heath/.../creative-and-hooks.md §Reels-first`)
- **Not a myth, an inconsistency:** Lorenzo banned "UGC testimonials" in 2024-11, then published a UGC template ending in a testimonial in 2026-03. Don't use either as a verdict.

## 8. Draper angle
PLAN gives none; a defensible one from the profile: **a review bank turns into many single-testimonial ads, and every one has to be launched.**
- The consensus says one customer per ad, so ten good lines become ten ads, each into every ad set it should test in. That's the "create once, deploy many" job. Do the arithmetic in the open (e.g. 10 testimonial ads × 4 ad sets × 17 clicks, stating it assumes a rebuild per ad set, or mark the duplicate cost `[TK]`).
- **Automatic ad naming** from the creative file: put the angle or objection in the file name (`price-objection_skeptic_v1`) so the later read shows which *kind* of testimonial wins. Links to 02.
- **Cloud-native creative**: raw customer and creator clips usually arrive in a shared Google Drive; launching straight from Drive skips the download/upload loop.
- Don't claim Draper finds testimonials, improves ROAS or runs partnership ads.
- Cross-links: 10 (UGC), 08 (statics: quote headline statics), 05 (fatigue: one testimonial wears out, a bank doesn't).

## 9. Open questions
- **Meta's before/after policy**, current wording: which categories and body areas are restricted, split-screen vs sequential, "personal attributes" rules. The corpus contradicts itself and parts are 2024-vintage.
- **Testimonial disclosure rules** (FTC endorsement guides; ASA in the UK) for paid creators, actors and staged "street interview" or "podcast" testimonials. The corpora are thin: Ben's corpus has no disclosure coverage, Alessio's is one sentence. Needs a primary source before the article says anything legal beyond "don't fake personal experience".
- **Meta's AI-content labelling** for synthetic people in ads.
- **Mock-review statics** (fake text threads, styled 1-star reviews): fine when the text is a real review; the article should say so. Confirm Meta's policy on UI mimicry.
- Candidate `[TK]`: one real before/after of a testimonial-led ad set from a Wonderland account (spend share of testimonial ads vs others). Only if Michael can supply it.
