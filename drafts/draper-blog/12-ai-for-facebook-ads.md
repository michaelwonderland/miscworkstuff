# AI for Facebook Ads: The Creative Workflow, Stage by Stage

Use AI for Facebook ads the way creative strategist Dara Denney does: as a junior strategist that gathers research, drafts copy, and varies what already works. A person still picks the angle, picks the winners, and signs off every ad. Skip full-AI ads that pretend to be real people.

That part is fairly settled. The part most guides skip comes next. **AI makes creative cheap, so the bottleneck moves to launching and analyzing it.** Say your team made 10 ads a week and AI takes it to 40. Ads Manager still wants every one of them built by hand.

We built Draper Ads inside a paid social agency, as the media buyers doing those uploads. Below: what AI does well and badly at each of six workflow stages, which of Meta's own AI features to switch on, the tools by job, and the math on the new bottleneck.

## How should you use AI for Facebook ads?

**Use AI to gather, draft, and vary. Keep the deciding human.**

Dara says AI only clicked for her when she "stopped using it as my replacement" and started treating it as a junior creative strategist or marketing assistant ([Claude Cowork video, May 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UGiN8aVy2l8)). She calls her own AI builds assistants or interns, never the head of strategy.

Her read on the market in January 2026 was blunter: "The hype around AI is starting to die and reality is really starting to settle in" ([AirPost video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gJrKogm75xg&t=488s)). Many teams that spent heavily on AI creative the year before found it didn't stick.

Her settled position on output came a month later. Full-AI ads are ["generally a disaster,"](https://x.com/DenneyDara/status/2018393739060744315) while the mixed media approach was producing consistent winners for her agency.

One definition before we go on, in our words. **Mixed media is creative that combines real footage, real people, or real product shots with parts that AI generated or assembled.**

And one rule has held through every phase of her AI views since 2024: humans are the editors. On any AI platform's output, her line is that she would ["never, ever"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5C5VhqW9HCc&t=1345s) call it ready to use.

So the question isn't whether to use AI. It's which stage you hand it, and what you do with the extra ads it makes.

## What AI does at each stage of the creative workflow

There are six stages between "we need new ads" and "we know which ones worked." AI helps most at the start, unevenly in the middle, and least at the end, which is where the work now piles up.

### 1. Research: let AI do the gathering

Dara uses Claude Cowork, the agent mode in Claude's desktop app, for research she used to do by hand every month. It breaks down a competitor's Ads Library, writes a weekly social performance report into Slack, and turns thousands of reviews into a persona deck.

Her worked example is Ridge Wallet: 291 active ads, four creator partners, a 4.5% partnership-ad share, and 94 ads launched in a single day. Hold on to that last number. It comes back later.

She also built an agent that writes a brand's reputation analysis from press, Reddit, reviews, and YouTube comments. By hand it takes her 3 to 4 hours per brand. Her reason for automating it is the interesting part: her manual version was ["incredibly biased to what I thought was the most important"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Uh0TE6nZs5A&t=183s).

**Where it hurts:** AI reports are confident and sometimes wrong. On camera, Cowork told her a post had gone viral. It hadn't; there was ad spend behind it. And Meta blocks AI tools from freely pulling Ads Library data, so an AI-only competitor audit works from copy and thumbnails rather than the full picture.

**The test:** would you trust this report if an intern wrote it? Then check it the way you'd check an intern's.

### 2. Strategy and personas: AI proposes, you decide

In Dara's [one-sitting research method](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yRgPbqywUJ8), an LLM reads the reviews, the reputation analysis, and a reference document, then drafts a persona deck. The goal is to find personas the account isn't reaching yet.

The next step stays manual. She compares the personas your current ads target with the personas your customers reveal, and the gaps fall out: personas nobody is talking to, a funnel skewed to bottom-of-funnel offers, too few angles per persona.

**Where it hurts:** ask an AI for strategy and it tends to hand back a summary of what's already common. Dara calls copying competitors "the worst creative strategy that you can do," and that includes picking from the Ads Library by impressions. She also flags demographic personas ("45+ women") as weaker than trigger-based ones (people whose problem started after a life event).

No model fixes the economics, either. In the same video she says you can't sell a $20 product on Meta, however good you are, because the costs are too high.

**The test:** for every planned ad, can a person on your team say why this persona, why this angle, and why this creative?

### 3. Briefs, copy, and hooks: vary what's proven

Copy is where AI earned its place first. In 2024 Dara rated the copy from Meta's gen-AI tools in Ads Manager as [comparable to her human teams', or better](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PmVv2i1UUWY), with the warning that humans need to edit.

Her current split, from [a July 2026 video on hiring](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gqnp-IdEEI8&t=733s), is ChatGPT or Poppy for copy and briefs, and Claude for reporting. Claude's copywriting, in her words, "kind of sucks." Treat that as a snapshot; model rankings change by the quarter.

Her most production-ready use is hooks. She uploads a brand's top five hooks and has Claude inject trigger words into structures that already convert. **The structure is proven; the AI only varies the words.** She tests [3 to 6 hook variants per video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I8tXqqfjIX4&t=551s).

Here's the difference in practice:

- **Weak prompt:** "Write 20 hooks for our deodorant."
- **Strong prompt:** "Here are our five best hooks by spend. Keep each structure. Swap in trigger words from these reviews, such as regret, embarrassed, and scam."

The strong prompt wins because it fences the AI inside what your account has already proved. The weak one returns the average of every deodorant ad it has read.

### 4. Video production: mixed media, not fake people

AI earns its keep in video when real footage sits underneath. Dara's agency uses AI voiceovers on client content "a lot." The one AI video service she has endorsed, AirPost, uses real actors and real studio footage, which AI then assembles into about 30 new ads a week for roughly $8K a month. A test on a friend's brand produced a $35 CPA, better than that brand had seen ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gJrKogm75xg)).

The other format that works is the opposite of realism: outrageous AI animated characters, which perform because they never pretend to be real.

**Where it hurts:** AI avatars posing as customers. In 2024 Dara said she avoids deepfake and UGC-clone tools ["for candidly ethical reasons."](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hvVPepEu7W8) She hasn't repeated that since, and she hasn't retracted it. When she later praised an AI campaign, it was for being ["honest AI,"](https://x.com/DenneyDara/status/1995587521217179720) not AI pretending to be real life.

We draw the same line, and our reason is commercial as much as ethical. **An AI avatar reading a testimonial is a testimonial from nobody.** A UGC ad persuades because a real person like the viewer tried the product; fake the person and you've removed the reason it works. Our guide to [what makes UGC ads work](/blog/what-are-ugc-ads) goes deeper on that.

**The test:** would the ad still feel honest if the viewer knew exactly how it was made?

### 5. Static ads: the fastest win, with a QA pass

Statics are where AI production is most usable today. They're cheaper to make than video, and they produce messaging lessons you can carry into video later.

Dara's pipeline, from a [June 2026 video sponsored by Super Scale](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5C5VhqW9HCc), runs in four moves:

1. Build a brand-brain document in Claude with a reusable prompt.
2. Load it into Super Scale, which pulls the brand from its URL and researches competitors in 2 to 5 minutes.
3. Review its gap analysis of missing personas, awareness levels, and formats.
4. Generate three variations at a time, then fix them with plain-language edits.

That editing loop, not the first draft, is why she rates the tool.

**Where it hurts:** clutter, spelling, and over-used formats. In the demo she rejected the tool's review-collage direction outright. And the category record is poor: after a month testing instant AI ad makers in 2025, her verdict was that ["none of these ad makers do the thing yet that they say they're going to do."](https://x.com/DenneyDara/status/1951003249202663645) Super Scale is a pressure-tested exception, not a reversal.

There's a platform trap too. Meta's Andromeda system [only counts visual difference as creative diversity](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kxLjzws-pNg&t=243s), per what Dara learned from Meta. Twenty AI variants of one layout with twenty headlines look like one ad to the algorithm. Her static tests run 20 to 25 ads per ad set with only 2 or 3 variations of any one concept. For layouts that are visually distinct, see our [static ads examples](/blog/static-ads-examples).

**The test:** can a stranger tell what you're selling within a second?

### 6. Launch and analysis: where AI helps least

AI can draft the readout. The launching and the deciding still land on a person.

For analysis, Dara calls Motion "non-negotiable" (disclosure: she is Motion's Chief Evangelist) and uses Runith to pull in-platform creative data into LLMs. Her rule for reading the numbers is strict: only [spend, purchases, CPA, and ROAS](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CCsty8R0UaA&t=670s) may trigger a scale or a pause. Hook rate and CTR explain; they never decide. An AI summary that recommends pausing an ad for low CTR breaks that rule, however well it's written.

Launching gets no help at all from a static generator. Every extra ad still needs a name, UTMs, placements, and a home in one or more ad sets. That's the stage the rest of this post is about.

## Meta's own AI ad tools: which to switch on

Most searches for "Meta AI ads" are about the features inside Ads Manager. Dara reviewed a batch of them after Meta's 2025 Performance Summit and a Cannes press briefing ([video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rqUuP-uc0SE&t=246s)). Her verdicts:

- **Persona-based text generation:** use it after you find winning copy, to adapt that copy for broader or narrower audiences.
- **Translation text generation:** the one she was most excited about. A founder on a panel she joined said ads in Spanish were a major growth lever for brands spending high six figures a year.
- **Brand-aware image generation:** uses your logo, fonts, and colors. She expects mostly new brands without a creative team to use it.
- **Video from multiple images:** worth a test with imagery that has already worked, especially for small teams.
- **Virtual try-on:** for apparel, showing clothes on different body sizes.
- **AI question bubbles on ads:** she's skeptical. They may signal "this is an ad" and pull people away from the comments, where trust gets built.

Two more notes from her. First, she suspects, unofficially, that Meta rewards early adopters of new gen-AI features, and says plainly that this isn't a Meta statement. Treat it as a hypothesis worth one test.

Second, check one setting today. Meta's AI-generated backgrounds for catalog ads were switched on by default for many accounts, and she calls the output a failure: ["Why is my couch SKU floating on a beach?"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AS0YfAioHT0&t=1402s)

## The launch gap: when making ads gets cheaper than launching them

**The launch gap is the distance between how many ads your team can make and how many it can launch, name, and read.** Before AI, production was the limit. Now it often isn't.

Start with the test sizes Dara uses today: [10 to 15 ads per video test ad set and 20 to 25 per static test](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MmXb2JmWDj8&t=121s), with 3 to 6 hooks per video. Those numbers assume you can produce that much. AI is what makes it possible.

Here's a hypothetical weekly test cadence for a two-person team:

| | Before AI | With AI |
|---|---|---|
| Video concepts × hooks | 2 × 4 = 8 | 3 × 5 = 15 |
| Statics | 8 | 20 |
| New ads per week | 16 | 35 |

Now launch them. Draper's figure for building one ad from scratch in Ads Manager is 17+ clicks. Thirty-five ads is 595 clicks a week, into one ad set each.

Winners rarely stay in one place. Say every ad also goes to a scaling campaign and an evergreen ad set, rebuilt each time, at a hypothetical 3 minutes per build:

- **One ad set:** 35 ads × 3 minutes = 105 minutes.
- **Three ad sets:** 315 minutes, or 5 hours 15 minutes a week.

Flex one variable. If the static generator doubles your statics to 40, you have 55 new ads, and the three-ad-set version takes 8 hours 15 minutes. That's a full working day of uploading, every week, for the person who should be reading the results.

Remember Ridge Wallet's 94 ads in one day? That's what this cadence looks like at full volume.

The reading suffers next. If half your ads are named "IMG_4471 copy," no report can group them by hook, persona, or format, and Dara's own reports compare exactly those things. A test you can't read was a waste of the AI time that made it.

**Volume isn't the goal, though.** In April 2026 Dara warned that many brands, ["in the name of creative diversity and volume,"](https://x.com/DenneyDara/status/2041506258008613177) were failing to double down on what was working. The strongest case for AI volume she cites, Monks' work for Headspace, made 460 assets in a third of the normal production time and lifted conversion rate 62%. Monks credited personalized creative across 20 use cases plus Meta's targeting, [not volume alone](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9SiRt3uvv-s&t=305s).

So the job is diverse ads, launched quickly, named so you can read them. That's the gap we built Draper to close: build the ad once, deploy it to every ad set, with names taken from the creative file, UTMs set once, and creative pulled straight from Google Drive. Teams using it report saving 16+ hours a week. [TK: one team's weekly launch time before and after Draper, with ad count]

If you launch a dozen ads a week, Ads Manager's duplicate button is fine. At 50, the launch gap is your bottleneck.

## AI tools for Facebook ads, by job

Pick tools by the job, not the demo. These are the tools Dara names, with her disclosures, plus our own:

| Job | Tools Dara names | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Research and reporting | Claude Cowork; Perplexity Computer for custom agents | Confident errors; limited Ads Library access |
| Copy and briefs | ChatGPT, Poppy | Generic hooks if the prompt isn't fenced |
| Static generation | Super Scale (sponsored her video) | Clutter and typos; review every output |
| Video assembly | AirPost (enterprise, about $8K a month) | Price; avoid avatar tools posing as customers |
| In-platform generation | Meta's gen-AI features in Ads Manager | Defaults switched on without notice |
| Swipe files | Foreplay | Inspiration, never the strategy |
| Creative analytics | Motion (she is its Chief Evangelist), Runith | Don't let storytelling metrics trigger kills |
| Sales and bundle data | Particle (one of her sponsors) | A complement to your own store data |
| Decks | Gamma (a partner of hers), Canva | None worth noting |
| Launching | None named; ours is Draper Ads | Ads Manager works at low volume |

Dara discloses her Motion role, her Gamma partnership, and her sponsors on camera. We do the same: we built Draper, so weigh the last row accordingly.

Her tool list is a mid-2026 snapshot. The jobs will outlast the names.

## Which AI setup fits your team?

The right amount of AI depends on who's doing the work:

- **A founder running their own ads:** start with Meta's text features and one chat model for hook variants. Make statics first. Check the economics before any of it; a $20 product won't be rescued by better creative.
- **A two- or three-person in-house team:** add a research agent for weekly reports and a static generator. You'll hit the launch gap first, so fix naming and launching before buying a third tool.
- **An agency:** the reputation-analysis agent saves 3 to 4 hours per new brand. Standard naming across every client account matters more than any generator.
- **Enterprise budgets:** a service like AirPost, at about $8K a month for around 30 ads a week, only pays off if your spend can absorb that many new ads.

## Recap

- **AI is a junior strategist:** it gathers, drafts, and varies. People choose angles, winners, and what ships.
- **Mixed media, not fake people:** real footage assembled by AI works; avatars posing as customers remove the reason UGC persuades.
- **Vary what's proven:** inject trigger words into converting hooks, generate statics three at a time, and make visual differences, not headline swaps.
- **Meta's AI features:** try translation and persona text; switch off AI catalog backgrounds unless you've checked them.
- **The launch gap:** once AI fills production, launching and reading become the limit. If your team spends more hours uploading ads than briefing them, fix launching before you buy another AI tool.
